# BTR DEX (AIMM) — Consolidated Security Audit

**Engagement:** Adversarial security review of the BTR DEX core (AIMM) and its oracle / signed-feed trust boundary.
**Date:** 2026-07-21
**Methodology:** 3-round multi-cohort review (independent discovery → adversarial cross-review → consolidation). Every candidate finding was debated and challenged; anything that could not survive the adversarial round was demoted to Info or moved to the refuted appendix.
**Disposition:** Read-only audit. No contract or test changes were made. NXR server-side (`~/Work/nx`) was treated as an adversarial input source, not audited.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The BTR DEX is a well-engineered, defense-in-depth adaptive multi-asset AMM. The signed-push oracle is cryptographically sound (EIP-712 domain binding, k-of-n distinct-signer quorum, monotonic replay guard, fail-closed staleness gate, byte-identical Rust/Solidity digest), and the pricing/accounting core is careful with rounding (pool-favoring), coverage haircuts, and reentrancy/flash guards. The assembly (blob parsing, digest scratch, slot packing) was reviewed and is correct.

**No Critical findings** survived the adversarial round. The residual risk is concentrated in two places:

1. **Operator/configuration trust is load-bearing and under-enforced.** Several economic safety rails — the cumulative mark-deviation band (`refBandBps`), the reservation-price ceiling (`reservationPriceMax`), and the anti-front-run relationship `minFee ≥ 2·θ` — are *optional* or *convention-only*. The shipped testnet volatile pools (BTC/ETH/WBNB/CAKE) run with the band disabled, which is exactly the configuration where the per-push deviation cap can self-widen under a compromised-quorum attack. These are the highest-value findings.
2. **Governance centralization.** A single beacon owns the entire live pool fleet; one timelocked owner action re-implements every pool atomically. The guardian veto is weaker than it looks because `setGuardian` is itself instant / un-timelocked.

The most important *correction* produced by this audit: a Round-1 claim that the σ-floor circuit-breaker makes an oracle-latency round-trip "spread-negative regardless of `minFee`" is **quantitatively false** — the σ→spread contribution is ≤ ~6.5% of the mark move. The real defense is the unenforced `minFee ≥ 2·θ` operator convention (Finding H-2).

## 4. High-severity findings

### H-1 — Beacon fleet upgrade is a single point of control; guardian veto is un-timelocked

**Severity:** High (centralization / governance)
**Location:** [`PoolFactory.sol:76-99`](file:///Users/derpa/Work/btr/dex/evm/src/PoolFactory.sol#L76), `executeReferenceUpgrade` / `requestReferenceUpgrade` / `cancelReferenceUpgrade`; Solady `UpgradeableBeacon`.

**Description.** Every pool proxy reads its implementation from one shared `UpgradeableBeacon` owned by the factory. `executeReferenceUpgrade → beacon.upgradeTo(newImpl)` swaps the implementation for the **entire live fleet atomically**. `_validateImplementation` checks only AC/admin/flash wiring and that aux is codeful — it does **not** verify storage-layout compatibility. The action is protected by a 7-day `UPGRADE_TIMELOCK` + grace window and a guardian/owner cancel path, but `setGuardian` is instant and un-timelocked, so an owner who controls the timelock can rotate the guardian immediately before/at execution, neutralizing the veto.

**Exploit scenario.** Owner-key compromise (or malicious governance) queues a fleet upgrade, and — because guardian rotation is instant — removes or replaces the guardian, letting the timelock mature and re-implementing every pool (drain, mint, disable withdrawals). Storage-layout drift in a *legitimate* upgrade could also corrupt every pool at once.

**Round-2 debate.** *Challenge:* "This is just standard owner trust." *Resolution:* Confirmed as a **concentration** issue, not an unconditional bug — it requires owner compromise/malice. It stays High because (a) blast radius is the whole fleet in one tx, (b) the guardian veto is materially weaker than the code comments imply due to instant `setGuardian`, and (c) `_validateImplementation` gives false assurance by not checking storage layout. The 7-day timelock is the real mitigation and is genuine.

**Recommended fix.**
- Timelock `setGuardian` (or make guardian a separate multisig that the owner cannot unilaterally rotate) so the veto cannot be removed inside an in-flight upgrade window.
- Add a storage-layout compatibility assertion (or an off-chain CI gate that is a hard release requirement) to `_validateImplementation`.
- Consider splitting the fleet across multiple beacons, or a per-pool opt-in/opt-out, to reduce single-tx blast radius.

### H-2 — Oracle-update front-run defense is an unenforced cross-contract convention

**Severity:** High (economic; High if misconfigured, Medium under recommended config)
**Location:** spread assembly in [`Pricing.sol` `_pathSpread`](file:///Users/derpa/Work/btr/dex/evm/src/libraries/Pricing.sol); minFee floor at [`PoolAdminWrite.sol:71,116`](file:///Users/derpa/Work/btr/dex/evm/src/libraries/PoolAdminWrite.sol#L71) (`minFeePbps < C.MIN_FEE_PBPS` is the *only* lower bound).

**Description.** When the keeper pushes a new mark, an informed trader who observes the pending mark update can trade against the *stale* on-chain mark before it lands (or immediately after, in the favorable direction). The system's protection against this is twofold: (1) the σ-floor, which floors stored σ at the realized `|Δmark|/mark` and thereby widens the spread; and (2) the fee. A Round-1 cohort claimed the σ-floor alone makes such a round-trip "spread-negative regardless of `minFee`."

**This claim is quantitatively false.** In `_pathSpread`, the σ contribution to the half-spread is `sigmaPair · max(vega) / (100 · BPS)`. With realistic `vega`, the σ→spread conversion factor is ≤ ~0.066, i.e. the σ-floor claws back only ~3–6.5% of the mark move `δ`. It cannot, by itself, make a `δ`-sized adverse mark move unprofitable to front-run. The **actual** defense is the operator convention `minFee ≥ 2·θ` (fee must exceed twice the per-leg edge). That relationship is **not enforced anywhere on-chain** — `minFeePbps` is only floored at `MIN_FEE_PBPS` (=1). Nothing prevents an operator from configuring a pool where `minFee < 2θ`, leaving it systematically extractable on every mark update.

**Exploit scenario.** For any pool where the operator sets a `minFee` below twice the effective edge (easy to do accidentally on a low-fee stable/volatile pair), a searcher extracts value from LPs on every keeper mark update by trading in the direction of the impending move, paying a fee smaller than the edge. Loss is continuous and scales with volume × update frequency.

**Round-2 debate.** *Challenge:* "The σ-floor makes this safe." *Resolution:* **Refuted** by re-deriving the arithmetic (Round 3 confirmed `vega/(100·BPS) ≤ 0.066`). The residual, correct finding is that the safety property depends on an **unenforced** parameter relationship. Severity is High if a pool is misconfigured, Medium under the recommended configuration (because it then requires an operator error rather than being always-on).

**Recommended fix.**
- Enforce `minFeePbps ≥ 2 · θ_effective` on-chain at `initAsset`/`setFee` (compute θ from `vega`/dispersion, or store an explicit floor), instead of relying on an operator convention.
- At minimum, add an invariant test and a deployment-time assertion that fails CI when any asset violates `minFee ≥ 2θ`.
- Document the σ-floor's true magnitude so future reviewers do not over-credit it.

## 5. Medium-severity findings

### M-1 — Cumulative mark deviation is unbounded when `refBandBps = 0`; σ-floor ratchet widens the per-push cap

**Severity:** Medium (High for the shipped volatile pools BTC/ETH/WBNB/CAKE, which ship with `refBandBps = 0`)
**Location:** [`ExternalOracle.sol` `_checkDeviation`](file:///Users/derpa/Work/btr/dex/evm/src/oracles/ExternalOracle.sol); `refBandBps`/`refPrimary` in [`IPool.sol:51-68`](file:///Users/derpa/Work/btr/dex/evm/src/interfaces/IPool.sol#L51) (`0 = disabled`); σ-floor in [`Oracle.markMovePbps`](file:///Users/derpa/Work/btr/dex/evm/src/libraries/Oracle.sol#L60).

**Description.** Each push is bounded *per-step* by `_checkDeviation`: `allowed = maxDev + DEV_SIGMA_Z(6)·σ·√(dt/interval)`, capped at `MAX_DEV_THRESHOLD` (65000 PBPS = 6.5%). The only bound on *cumulative* drift over many pushes is the optional reference-band depeg breaker (`refBandBps` against `refPrimary`). That band is `0 = disabled` by default, and the shipped volatile pools disable it. Worse, the σ-floor creates a ratchet: a large mark move floors the stored σ upward, which *widens* the very `allowed` band that gates the *next* push (because `allowed` is linear in σ). Under a compromised quorum, the per-push cap self-widens as the attacker walks the mark.

**Exploit scenario.** With `refBandBps = 0` and a compromised k-of-n signer quorum, an attacker walks the mark in a sequence of pushes; each large step raises σ, which raises the next push's allowed deviation, accelerating the walk. There is no cumulative ceiling to arrest it, so the mark can be driven arbitrarily far from truth (subject only to `ttl`/push cadence), then drained against. Even absent a full quorum compromise, a single faulty/hostile source that clears the per-push band can drift the mark materially over time on these pools.

**Round-2 debate.** *Challenge:* "Requires quorum compromise, so it's just a keeper-trust issue." *Resolution:* **Confirmed Medium.** The per-push cap and monotonic replay guard are strong; the gap is specifically the *missing cumulative bound* plus the *σ-floor→band-widening interaction* (finding N-1), which degrades the per-push cap precisely when it matters. It is High for the shipped volatile pools because they concretely ship with the mitigating band disabled.

**Recommended fix.**
- Require a non-zero `refBandBps` (or an absolute cumulative-drift ceiling / TWAP sanity bound) for EXTERNAL-mode volatile pools; reject `refBandBps = 0` at config time for assets flagged volatile.
- Decouple the deviation band from the *floored* σ: compute `allowed` from the *attested* σ (or a governance-set σ cap), not the ratcheted stored σ, so an attacker cannot widen their own gate.
- Add an absolute per-epoch cumulative-move cap independent of σ.

### M-2 — RiskSteward relative clamp has no cooldown → multi-tx ratchet

**Severity:** Medium
**Location:** `AdminRiskSteward.sol` (relative-clamp path).

**Description.** The RiskSteward may adjust risk parameters within a bounded *relative* clamp per call (e.g. ±X% of current), but there is no cooldown between calls. A steward key can therefore apply the clamp repeatedly in consecutive transactions to reach a value far outside the intended single-step envelope, defeating the purpose of the relative bound.

**Exploit scenario.** A compromised or overreaching steward ratchets a parameter (e.g. reservation-price ceiling, fee, dispersion) step-by-step across many txs within one block/several blocks to an extreme value the clamp was meant to prevent in a single move, then exploits the resulting mispricing.

**Round-2 debate.** *Challenge:* "Steward is trusted and hard-capped by the hard-min/hard-max ceilings." *Resolution:* **Confirmed Medium.** The absolute hard-min/hard-max (`minFeeHardMin/Max`, `reservationHardHiMax`) do cap the terminal value, so it is not unbounded — but between those absolute ceilings the relative clamp provides *no* real protection without a cooldown, which is its stated intent.

**Recommended fix.** Add a per-parameter cooldown (min time between steward adjustments) and/or a cumulative-move accumulator that decays over time, so the relative clamp cannot be defeated by rapid repetition.

### M-3 — `setBaseToken` strands sibling spokes (routing DoS)

**Severity:** Medium
**Location:** `PoolAdminWrite.setBaseToken` + [`PoolFactory.setPoolBaseToken`](file:///Users/derpa/Work/btr/dex/evm/src/PoolFactory.sol#L137); base-must-be-EXTERNAL check at [`PoolAdminWrite.sol:250`](file:///Users/derpa/Work/btr/dex/evm/src/libraries/PoolAdminWrite.sol#L250).

**Description.** The pool is a star topology with a single base numeraire; all spokes route through the base. Migrating the base via `setBaseToken` re-points the anchor, but existing spokes priced/anchored against the old base can be left unroutable (their anchor path no longer resolves), stranding liquidity and breaking `checkRoute`/`getCommonPools` for those pairs until each is re-configured.

**Exploit scenario.** An admin base migration (even a legitimate one) silently breaks routing for a subset of spokes; users cannot swap those pairs and LP exits for stranded spokes may be forced through degraded paths. Not attacker-driven, but a foot-gun with fund-availability impact.

**Round-2 debate.** *Challenge:* "Admin-only, so operational risk not a vuln." *Resolution:* **Confirmed Medium** as an availability/foot-gun issue: the operation succeeds without validating that all existing spokes remain routable against the new base, so the damage is silent and discovered only post-hoc.

**Recommended fix.** In `setBaseToken`, require the new base to be reachable from (or explicitly re-anchor) every registered spoke, or block the migration while spokes remain anchored to the old base; emit a per-spoke re-anchor requirement and gate trading on completion.

### M-4 — Asymmetric freeze/unfreeze can lock funds

**Severity:** Medium (mitigated by revocable guardian)
**Location:** halt gating via `PoolIO.checkRiskFlags(rc, 0)` on both withdraw endpoints ([`PoolLiquidity.sol:155-156`](file:///Users/derpa/Work/btr/dex/evm/src/libraries/PoolLiquidity.sol#L155)); `HALT_MASK = FROZEN_BIT | ASSET_PAUSED_BIT`.

**Description.** `withdrawTo` gates on `HALT_MASK` for **both** endpoints (correct, and confirmed — this closes the guardian-freeze-bypass that the code comments describe). The consequence is that a frozen/paused asset also blocks *withdrawals*, so if a guardian freeze is set and not lifted (guardian key lost, or freeze/unfreeze authority asymmetric), user funds in that asset are locked. The freeze is a legitimate safety control, but the asymmetry between who/what can freeze vs. unfreeze creates a fund-lock surface.

**Exploit scenario.** Guardian (halt-only) freezes an asset during an incident; the owner path to unfreeze is delayed/unavailable, or the guardian key is compromised to freeze-and-hold. LPs cannot withdraw the frozen asset for the duration.

**Round-2 debate.** *Challenge:* "Withdrawals should stay open during a freeze." *Resolution:* **Refuted** — gating withdrawals is *correct* (otherwise a freeze is bypassable by draining the halted asset or pricing a good asset off a compromised feed). The real, narrower finding is **fund-lock risk from freeze/unfreeze asymmetry**, Medium, and materially mitigated because the guardian is owner-revocable and freeze is intended to be temporary.

**Recommended fix.** Add a maximum freeze duration / auto-expiry (freeze lapses after N days unless re-asserted), and ensure the unfreeze authority is at least as available as the freeze authority. Emit freeze timestamps so monitoring can alarm on stale freezes.

## 6. Low-severity and Info findings

### L-1 — `maxRelayLagSecs` (120s) << `ttl` (600s): stale-but-valid pick-off
`ExternalOracle` accepts a signed blob if relay lag ≤ `maxRelayLagSecs` (120s on the reviewed testnet config), but the feed remains *fresh* for reads until `ttl` (600s). A relayer who withholds a signed blob and lands it late (but within 120s of source time) can still pick a favorable moment; more importantly, if the keeper gaps, reads stay valid for up to 600s against a mark that may be materially stale. **Low** (testnet param; needs a keeper gap or withholding relayer). *Fix:* align read-freshness with relay-lag expectations, or lower `ttl` for volatile pairs.

### L-2 — `UniPoolOracle` spot/`slot0` read + disabled staleness (latent)
`UniPoolOracle` reads `slot0` spot (manipulable within a block) and has staleness effectively disabled. It is not wired into the shipped pools, but there is **no on-chain guard preventing it from being registered** as a primary/ref oracle. **Low now / High-if-misused.** *Fix:* use a TWAP (min observation window) rather than `slot0`; add a registration guard that rejects spot-only oracles for pricing roles.

### L-3 — YieldHook withdrawal DoS via venue-recall revert (bounded)
`withdrawTo` calls `PoolHooks.preOutflow` (venue recall) before the ledger debit; if the yield venue reverts on recall, the withdrawal reverts. Bounded (funds not lost, only temporarily unavailable; recall failure is a venue-liveness dependency). The phantom-credit NAV-manipulation variant was **refuted** (keeper-gated, 1% cap, socialized, unprofitable). **Low-Med.** *Fix:* allow a degraded-mode withdrawal path (partial/liquid-only) when a venue recall fails.

### L-4 — JIT liquidity: `flowCooldownSeconds = 0` disables the cooldown
`_checkCooldown` early-returns when `flowCooldownSeconds == 0` ([`PoolLiquidity.sol:19-27`](file:///Users/derpa/Work/btr/dex/evm/src/libraries/PoolLiquidity.sol#L19)). With cooldown disabled (a valid config), a JIT LP can deposit, capture fees/rewards on a known incoming swap, and withdraw same-block. **Low-Med.** *Fix:* enforce a non-zero minimum cooldown, or a same-block deposit→withdraw guard, for fee-earning assets.

### L-5 — Skew bucket path-dependence → directional slippage optimization
`computeInventorySkew` quantizes to ~1% buckets and `_traverseCurve` computes `volumeFraction` per-call rather than cumulatively, so splitting a directional trade across bucket boundaries yields marginally better fills than a single trade. The round-trip (there-and-back) arbitrage was **refuted** in Round 2 — this is only a slippage optimization for one-directional flow, not free value extraction. **Low.** *Fix:* accept as by-design, or compute impact on cumulative volume within a tx.

### L-6 — On-chain confidence has no floor
Confidence (CI) is attested and gated only at the halt ceiling (`MAX_CONFIDENCE_HALT_BPS = 1000`); there is no lower floor, and its spread contribution is bounded by `maxFeePath` and is non-additive. Exploiting requires quorum compromise. **Low/Info.** *Fix:* none required; document.

### L-7 — ChainlinkOracle has no L2 sequencer-uptime feed
`ChainlinkOracle` does not consult an L2 sequencer-uptime feed; on an L2, just-resumed-sequencer conditions could serve a stale-but-"fresh" price. **Low/Info** for the current deployment target. *Fix:* add the sequencer-uptime check if deploying to Arbitrum/Optimism-style L2s.

### L-8 — `AdminRisk.batch` silently skips invalid legs
The batch risk-config path silently skips legs that fail validation rather than reverting. Detectable (events differ) and an atomic single-leg path exists, so partial application is recoverable. **Low.** *Fix:* revert on any invalid leg, or return an explicit per-leg status.

### L-9 — `Admin._emitQueued` has no already-pending guard
`Admin._emitQueued` can emit a queued event for an op that is already pending, potentially confusing off-chain timelock trackers. (Note: `PoolFactory.requestReferenceUpgrade` *does* guard `pendingReferencePool != 0`.) **Low.** *Fix:* guard against re-queueing an already-pending op.

### Info
- **Dust / truncation:** sub-depth-fraction dust trades round to zero impact; deposits below 1 LP share are rejected (`ACC-03`); haircut rounds up (`ACC-04`, pool-favoring). All by-design and pool-favoring.
- **`flashFee` returns a fee for non-borrowable tokens:** view-only cosmetic; `flashLoan` still reverts for non-borrowable tokens.
- **Permissionless `createPool` pollution:** bounded by `MAX_POOL_TOKENS=32` / `MAX_TOKEN_POOLS=128`; official discovery index is unpolluted; owner `deregisterPool` evicts griefing clones.
- **Keeper pre-broadcast omits σ/deviation checks:** liveness-only; on-chain verification is authoritative.
- **First push bounded to admin seed:** the initial mark is bounded by the admin-seeded value; Info.

## 7. Systemic / architectural observations

1. **Trust model is operator-heavy by design.** The oracle is a signed-push external mark; correctness depends on (a) honest k-of-n keepers, (b) correct per-asset configuration (`refBandBps`, `reservationPriceMax`, `minFee ≥ 2θ`), and (c) honest timelocked governance. The cryptography and fail-closed gating are strong; the residual risk is almost entirely in *configuration enforcement* (H-2, M-1) and *governance concentration* (H-1). The single highest-leverage improvement is to **promote load-bearing operator conventions into on-chain invariants**.

2. **Defense-in-depth is real and well-executed.** Fail-closed staleness gate (STALE/DEAD/UNCERTAIN triad), monotonic replay guard, distinct-signer quorum via strictly-increasing recovered address, pool-favoring rounding, flash-inflight reentrancy flag, HALT_MASK gating on value-moving entrypoints (including cross-asset withdraw), and depeg breakers on both legs of a cross withdraw. These are correct.

3. **Assembly / EVM correctness is clean.** Blob parsing, EIP-712 digest scratch usage, packed-slot reads, and TransientCache tx+pool+token scoping were reviewed and are correct. Only two cosmetic comment nits were found (no code impact).

4. **Cross-language parity is locked.** The Rust keeper `batch_digest` reconstructs the EIP-712 domain (chainId + verifying contract) and record layout byte-identically to the Solidity assembly, and a cross-impl cast fixture (`FIX_DIGEST`) pins the parity in tests. ecrecover `address(0)` rejection and s-malleability are handled.

## 9. Recommendations (prioritized)

1. **(H-2)** Enforce `minFee ≥ 2·θ` on-chain at `initAsset`/`setFee`; add a CI/deploy assertion. *Highest leverage — removes a silent, always-on LP-drain surface.*
2. **(M-1)** Require non-zero `refBandBps` (or an absolute cumulative-drift/TWAP ceiling) for EXTERNAL volatile pools; compute the deviation band from *attested* (not floored) σ to kill the ratchet.
3. **(H-1)** Timelock `setGuardian`; add a storage-layout compatibility gate to `_validateImplementation`; consider multi-beacon fleet partitioning.
4. **(M-4)** Add a freeze auto-expiry and ensure unfreeze availability ≥ freeze availability.
5. **(M-2)** Add a per-parameter cooldown / decaying accumulator to the RiskSteward relative clamp.
6. **(M-3)** Validate spoke routability in `setBaseToken` before allowing base migration.
7. **(L-2, L-4, L-1)** Add a registration guard rejecting spot-only oracles for pricing roles; enforce a minimum non-zero `flowCooldownSeconds` for fee-earning assets; align read-freshness with relay-lag on volatile pairs.

Entrypoints enforce `minAmountOut` but accept no `deadline`, so a signed tx can be held in the mempool and included later, back-running an oracle mark push while still clearing a loose `minAmountOut`. ### 11.2 New regression introduced by the fix batch (Round G)

**R-1 — Raw B64 comparison in reservation-band validation/steward paths (the team's own decode fix was not propagated)** — **Low** (impact: silent acceptance of an inverted band → asset-wide swap DoS, or rejection of a legitimate cross-decade band; privileged owner/steward-gated; only bites near a `4.5×10^k` mantissa-wrap boundary).

B64 packs the mantissa in the **high bits** (`packed = shl(12, mant) | …`, [B64.sol:43](file:///Users/derpa/Work/btr/shared/evm/src/libs/B64.sol#L43)), so a raw `uint64` `<`/`>` orders by mantissa first and is **non-monotonic with the decoded value across a decade boundary**. The enforcement path was fixed to decode-before-compare and even carries a warning comment ([PoolIO.priceBandGuard:174-179](file:///Users/derpa/Work/btr/dex/evm/src/libraries/PoolIO.sol#L174-L179)), but the same fix was **not mirrored** to the config/steward validation paths:
- [`PoolAdminWrite.setAssetParams:128`](file:///Users/derpa/Work/btr/dex/evm/src/libraries/PoolAdminWrite.sol#L128) — `reservationPriceMax < reservationPrice` on raw B64 (lead-confirmed).
- `AdminRiskSteward._enforceHard` / `_narrowsReservation` / `_relOk` — hard-fence, narrowing, and relative-clamp comparisons all on raw B64.

*Counterexample:* `4.0` encodes to mantissa `4.0e15`; `5.0` renormalizes to mantissa `5.0e14` (exp+1), so `raw(4.0) > raw(5.0)` although `4.0 < 5.0`. The inverted-band guard at :128 therefore fails to fire for `(min=5.0, max=4.0)`, and `priceBandGuard` (which decodes correctly) then reverts every swap on that asset. `_relOk` fails *safe* (over-restrictive across a boundary), so the steward cannot *widen* a breaker past the hard fence — which is why this is Low, not higher. Standard stable bands clustered near `1.0` never cross a boundary and are unaffected.

*Fix:* route every reservation-bound comparison through the decoded-1e18 comparator (`M.b64To1e18`) exactly as `priceBandGuard` does — `PoolAdminWrite.sol:128`, `AdminRiskSteward._enforceHard`/`_narrowsReservation`/`_relOk`.

**Everything else in the fix-touched surfaces verified SOUND** (Round G, lead-spot-checked): `minFee` floor enforced on both setters with no bypass; mandatory breaker for INTERNAL + EXTERNAL spokes correctly re-asserted after a band-zeroing `setAssetParams`; `deadline` check inclusive and applied on all price-sensitive entrypoints; guardian remains **instant** for all halt/tighten/cancel powers (no liveness regression from the timelock work) and owner-revocable; deviation band reads stored (not attacker-attested) σ and is cap-bounded with correct unit math; `raiseIndex` index invariant and pool-favorable rounding intact; no buy-leg Newton loop was added (no convergence/gas risk); no freeze auto-expiry exists (so no auto-unfreeze-into-incident risk); no storage-layout change that would break the beacon fleet; Solidity↔Rust oracle record layout still byte-identical and fixture-locked.
