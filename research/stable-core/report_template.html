7 · The answer — faithful state-machine simulation (the definitive number)

Every swap replayed through a live model of our own pool

Not a static estimate. A live PoolState (shared USDC base + per-spoke reserves) processes 11.5M swaps in time order: the real quote law prices each trade at the current coverage with a live mark tracked from the tape; if our output beats the trade's actual fill we win it, apply the reserve deltas (coverage moves), and the keeper rebalances at market cost when a leg leaves the 0.8–1.2 band. This prices in execution-competition, inventory skew, and rebalancing cost together.

What this means for launch

Real but modest. We win ~20% of trades / 14–20% of volume — the looser-executed slice — on our fresh-mark edge, for ~$80–140k/yr in fees and mid-single-digit-to-teens net APR (higher at small TVL where the fixed fee base concentrates). We do not beat the ~0bp PMM/CLMM venues (Infinity, Topaz) — their execution sits at fair and our 0.6bp spread can't undercut it.

Why the earlier numbers were wrong: a fee-tier decode bug made those venues look free (they're not — it's execution), a flat 1.0 mark mispriced us by more than our spread (the peg sits at ~1.0004), and the static replay ignored coverage skew. Fixing all three — price-based competition, live marks from the tape, and a faithful inventory state machine — is what produced the numbers above.

Read: BSC stable-core is a credible positioning / loss-leader product (own the "cheapest + safest-execution" narrative), not a revenue engine. Our structural edge — fresh-mark LVR recapture — pays off far more in volatile pairs (high σ), where fees aren't zero and passive AMMs bleed to arbs.
